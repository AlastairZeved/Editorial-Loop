## Edited Text (single pass, general-purpose editing)

The real issue with vibe coding isn't the quality of the code -- it's the gap between what you *think* you asked for and what the AI actually built. When you describe what you want in plain English, the AI doesn't understand your words the way a human colleague would. Instead, it pattern-matches against millions of code examples it was trained on and assembles something that *looks* like what you described. Your simple request passes through several layers of interpretation before it becomes working code -- and something can get lost at every layer.

This is why how you phrase your request matters so much. The gap between what you meant and what the AI produced is where problems hide -- not in typos or broken syntax, but in misunderstandings. Think of it like writing a legal contract: every vague or ambiguous phrase in your description becomes a place where things can go wrong.

An experienced developer knows to review the generated code and catch these misunderstandings. But if you're coming to this fresh, you're essentially signing a contract you can't read. And unlike a bad legal contract, a bad piece of code won't just sit there quietly -- it will run. Every time. Doing exactly what it was written to do, not what you intended it to do.
